## What Neel's taste means for your scoring
1. **Poetic beats literal.** His favourite lines put an accurate idea inside an image: "you packed five secrets into two, and never let them show" (superposition); "the N-L-A read me what you thought but never said / but some of what it read me was a letter it wrote instead" (NLAs are useful but confabulate). Reward images that are both beautiful and true.
2. **Nothing on the nose.** A specific name or number that can't be worked in naturally ("Wood Labs", "thirty-four million, two-thirds dead") belongs in the video background, not the lyric. Test every line: would Neel, an expert, follow it without the footnote? He did not follow "the N-L-A read your sibling". Would a normie at least enjoy its surface? List failures as `on_the_nose_lines`. Some specifics do sing naturally ("a plain old probe hit point-nine-nine-nine", "subtract the awareness: eight percent expressed" were praised), so judge case by case.
3. **One coherent narrative.** One story, one frame, carried all the way through. Each section should be the next beat of that story, not a new exhibit. "A bit too all over the place" is a real criticism.
4. **Storyline and flow over density.** A line whose main job is to name a paper, tool or number is shoehorned. It is better to leave a reference out than to shoehorn it. The round-2 asks above are a menu the story should mostly cover, not a checklist.
5. **No self-quotes.** Neel's own words sung or spoken as a sample read as "on the nose / narcissistic". A job-security joke in the narrator's own words is fine.
6. **Variation.** Round 2's five converged on the same arc order and the same stock lines. Those stock lines are listed at the end of the anchors; penalise a new song that leans on them.

## The bar: "I'm Upping My P(doom)"
The project's model is a viral song (a love song sung to the AI, infatuation lurching into dread; a fixed hook with a rotating payload; short tight couplets; a ladder of obscurity where every line works on the surface).

## Craft principles
- **One device, carried through:** a relationship, a frame or a question that turns each reference into plot.
- **Rolling chorus:** a fixed anchor line, with new rhyme partners that mark the story's progress.
- **Cliché swaps:** a stock pop phrase with one technical double meaning.
- **Ladder of obscurity:** a normie can sing along; an expert catches the layers.
- **Killing part:** a 1–2 bar standout people will clip and replay.
- **Singability by an AI singer:** stressed, sustained notes for key words; spellable acronyms (N-L-A); numbers as words; scansion.
- **Length:** a tight pop song, roughly 2:45–3:30. Padding is a flaw.

## Scoring rubric (each 1–10, integers)
Calibration: B as Neel saw it ("What's On Your Mind?", round 2) scores roughly poetry 7, story 5, naturalness 6, hook 7, overall 7. A ("Don't Go Quiet On Me", round 2) scores roughly poetry 6, story 7, naturalness 5, hook 7, overall 7. A 5 is "competent, forgettable"; a 10 is "I'd bet this goes viral".
1. **story**: one coherent narrative with flow. Does each section follow from the last inside one frame? (Not "all over the place".)
2. **poetry**: imagery and craft. Are ideas carried by images, as in the gold lines, rather than by stating them?
3. **naturalness**: no on-the-nose specifics. Does every reference sit in the lyric naturally? (10 = nothing needs a footnote.)
4. **hook**: catchiness of the title, hook and chorus.
5. **accuracy**: every technical claim is correct and fairly characterised, even when it's poetic. Flag anything doubtful.
6. **singability**: scansion, stress, rhyme, syllable counts, and how an AI singer would handle the jargon.
7. **humour**: genuinely funny and clever.
8. **heart**: an emotional arc. Does the darker ending land?
9. **neel_fit**: affectionate, clear-eyed, both halves of the field, no self-quotes, punches at ideas not people.
10. **feedback_fit**: how well it follows Neel's round-2 verdict above. For A' and B', did it apply his specific notes? For new songs, did it follow the general notes?
11. **overall**: your holistic judgement of how good the final song could be (not an average).
